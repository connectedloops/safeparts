Safeparts reduces single points of failure. It does not choose safe holders, protect your storage locations, or stop anyone with enough Recovery shares from recovering the Secret.

## What to assume

- Anyone with enough valid shares from the same split can recover the secret.
- A smaller group should not learn useful information about the secret.
- If too many shares are lost, recovery is impossible.
- A passphrase adds another requirement, but it does not fix a weak storage plan.

In a 3-of-5 plan, for example, any three valid shares can recover. Two shares are not enough, and the plan can still survive two unavailable holders.

For support requests and audit records, use synthetic examples rather than live shares, passphrases, or recovered output.

## Storage and transport rules

Keep fewer Recovery shares than the Threshold in every account, device, location, administrator domain, and transport channel. Apply this rule both while storing Recovery shares and while moving them to their custodians. A single Recovery share still needs the same careful handling as the Secret.

<Steps>
1. Choose independent custodians and control boundaries before you split the Secret.
2. Check that no vault, safe, drive, ticket, repository, CI secret store, administrator, or delivery channel can collect enough Recovery shares to meet the Threshold.
3. Keep a custody record that names the holder or role for each Recovery share without storing its text.
4. Practice recovery with synthetic data, then complete the [saved-backup checkpoint](../it-devops-guide/break-glass/#verify-saved-backup) on the actual custody copies before retiring the working copy or declaring the backup established. The drill alone cannot validate those copies.
5. Rotate the underlying Secret after operational recovery or suspected exposure. A controlled setup verification follows the checkpoint's custody and cleanup rules; if those controls fail, rotate and verify a fresh backup.
</Steps>

Splitting initially creates all Recovery shares in the controlled setup environment. Verification and recovery also require temporary aggregation of at least the Threshold. Authorize and limit these sessions, then remove temporary material and restore separated custody. This is not permission to retain enough shares in one account, device, administrator domain, or transport channel. Keep the known-good working copy if setup verification fails; correct or recreate the backup and verify again before proceeding.

## Passphrases

When you add a passphrase, Safeparts encrypts the secret before splitting it. Recovery then requires both enough shares and the passphrase.

Use a passphrase when shares may travel through higher-risk environments, when share holders should not be enough on their own, or when policy requires a knowledge factor.

Keep the passphrase separate from the shares. If the same person, vault, or device can provide both the passphrase and enough shares, the extra protection is mostly procedural.

## Local execution boundaries

The web app performs split and combine in your browser through WASM. The desktop app uses local Tauri commands over `safeparts_core`. The CLI and TUI run locally on your machine.

Safeparts does not upload secrets by default. Secrets can still leak if you paste them into another service, use a modified build, save them to an unsafe location, or let logs and screen recorders capture them.

### Browser writing assistance

The web app asks browsers to disable spellchecking, autocorrection, and automatic capitalization in Secret, Recovery-share, and passphrase fields. These settings reduce exposure to writing-assistance services and help keep your input unchanged.

They do not guarantee a trustworthy browser, extension environment, or clipboard. Safeparts cannot control extensions or all browser-provider features. Use a browser and device you trust, review writing-assistance settings, and avoid clipboard sync or history for sensitive text. This mitigation does not mean that a disclosure was observed.

## If something goes wrong

- If one share is lost, check whether enough shares still remain. If yes, recover, rotate the underlying secret, and create a new split.
- If one share may have been copied, treat that share as compromised.
- If enough shares may have been copied to meet the threshold, treat the original secret as compromised.
- If recovery happens on an untrusted machine, rotate the recovered credential as soon as possible.
